§ 03

Recent Reads

learn with me!
Pharma · Investigative
Bottle of Lies
Katherine Eban
"data fraud doesn't look like fraud — it looks like paperwork"

Eban's investigation into generic drug manufacturing taught me that regulatory failure isn't dramatic — it's incremental, buried in falsified lab records and intimidated inspectors. Essential reading for anyone who thinks healthcare data is neutral.

Technology · Geopolitics
Chip War
Chris Miller
"the most important industry in the world runs on physics and politics"

A reminder that the hardware underlying every analysis I run has a supply chain more fragile than most people realize. Understanding tech at the systems level — not just the code level — shapes how I think about infrastructure risk in healthcare data.

Neuroscience · Memoir
The Mind's Eye
Oliver Sacks
"the brain doesn't just process the world — it constructs it"

Sacks writing about his own vision loss while studying perception disorders is the most personal neuroscience I've read. It deepened my understanding of neural plasticity and reminded me why I study the brain — because individual variation is where the real science lives.

Fiction · AI · Ethics
Klara and the Sun
Kazuo Ishiguro
"what does it mean to observe carefully and still be wrong?"

Klara is an artificial friend who observes humans with extraordinary precision and still fundamentally misunderstands them. As someone building models of human health data, that tension — between measurement and meaning — stays with me.

Medicine · Memoir
The Tennis Partner
Abraham Verghese
"medicine is a relationship before it is a science"

Verghese's account of friendship, addiction, and medicine in a residency program is a reminder that the patients in my NHANES dataset are people with full lives. Healthcare analytics without that awareness produces technically correct answers that miss the point.
